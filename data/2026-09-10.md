### [3] [Reducing Prescription Errors Through Information Intervention: A Field Experiment in Healthcare Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09673)

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: DDI错误严重威胁患者安全。现有决策支持系统通常要求医生对警报作出响应，干扰工作流程并导致高忽略（override）率，因此需要检验一种不增加响应负担的信息干预能否有效减少错误并促进学习。

Method: 采用随机田野实验，依托印度最大的电子病历平台，覆盖1700名医生的281万张处方；使用双重差分（difference-in-differences）设计。处理组医生接收实时DDI错误提示但无需强制响应，对照组不接收此类信息。

Conclusion: 非强制性信息干预可通过即时纠错与持久、可泛化的学习两方面提升患者安全，为替代高负担强制警报型决策支持提供了可行路径。

We identify two mechanisms: reactive correction, whereby physicians remove errors after they are flagged, and proactive learning, whereby they avoid errors before alerts occur. While early reductions are driven primarily by correction, physicians increasingly avoid errors over time. They also become less likely to repeat previously flagged errors and reduce new errors, suggesting that learning generalizes beyond specific drug pairs. The effects are consistent across physician types and do not compromise productivity or care quality. Our findings show that non-mandatory information interventions can improve patient safety through both immediate error correction and persistent, generalizable learning.

  <summary>Details</summary>
