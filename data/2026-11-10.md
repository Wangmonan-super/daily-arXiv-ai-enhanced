Conclusion: 公务员工资的间断式调整可由固定调整成本与凸性融资成本的相互作用及财政状态内生解释；司法审查的时点具有实质福利含义——事前审查（在削减生效前）相较事后审查可降低葡萄牙危机的社会成本；部分指数化在孟加拉本可改善结果，除非其自动加薪的融资成本过高。政策含义是制度设计（审查时序、指数化规则）而非单次立法决定，决定工资调整的效率与社会成本。

Abstract: Public pay is set by law in nominal terms, so inflation erodes its real value between legal acts until the government revises it. I model each revision as the choice of a forward-looking government, shaped by two costs that push in opposite directions. A fixed cost of acting makes revisions large and rare, whereas a convex cost of financing the increase at once makes them small and frequent. Through the budget, each revision also moves debt and the risk of fiscal stress under which the next one is made. In the documented pay decisions of eleven countries, pay set at discretion erodes against prices and private earnings and is restored in discrete steps, every cut was decided during market stress or an adjustment program, and court rulings drove a wedge between the cuts legislated, the cuts paid and the cuts that lasted. Estimated on the legal acts, the model reproduces the documented paths of pay in most countries when data and simulations are measured the same way. In Portugal's estimated model, the savings that the Treasury requires once spreads rise produce the cuts, the debt overhang holds pay down after the crisis, and a court that strikes cuts after they take effect invites cuts it will strike. Review of cuts before they took effect would have lowered the social cost of Portugal's crisis relative to review after the fact, and partial indexation would have paid in Bangladesh unless its automatic increases were costly to finance.
